# Editorial Principles

Use this reference when the draft needs a deeper editorial pass than the main skill body provides.

## People-First Standard

Before style, make the writing useful. A strong piece should answer:

1. What happened?
2. Why does it matter?
3. Who is affected?
4. What changed?
5. What evidence supports the claim?
6. What should the reader understand, decide, or do next?

## Lexical Register And Accessibility

This is the word-choice layer of the draft:

- how concrete or abstract the language is
- how formal or conversational the tone feels
- whether jargon is earned
- whether the piece sounds readable to the intended audience

Prefer language that feels natural for a practitioner review, not a branding exercise.

## Practitioner Vocabulary

Concrete words usually beat prestige language.

Prefer terms like:

- pipeline
- queue
- threshold
- migration
- reviewer
- sample
- rollout
- failure mode
- latency
- handoff
- validation

Only use elevated or promotional language when the genre truly requires it.

## Diagnostic Questions

When a draft still feels off, check:

- Is the main claim supported or only decorated?
- Did the writer describe a mechanism or only a benefit?
- Could a teammate say this sentence in a project review without sounding unnatural?
- Is the vocabulary helping comprehension or signaling status?
